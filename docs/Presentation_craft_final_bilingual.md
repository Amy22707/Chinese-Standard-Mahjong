# 国标麻将报告：中英文讲稿

## 第 1 页：标题

大家好，我是来自北京大学的薛之瑶。接下来由我为大家介绍一下我的Bot。

## 第 2 页：项目的基础架构

先介绍项目的基础架构，这也是参加积分赛的bot的结构。训练数据来自2020年比赛的冠军 Bot的自对弈牌谱。每个决策局面被编码成一个 $70\times4\times9$ 的张量，其中 $4\times9$ 是麻将牌的网格结构；输入特征包括手牌和剩余牌、四家牌河与副露、牌墙进度、逐对手危险度、弃牌时序位置、向听数与有效进张，以及特殊番型路线。

网络使用 ResNet 提取牌型组合和局面上的公开特征，再通过分头策略预测动作类型和具体牌张，最后在 235 维动作空间上应用合法动作掩码，保证输出能够直接执行。
## 第 3 页：为什么普通行为克隆不够

然而，由积分赛到决赛的bot性能提升依旧存在瓶颈。在前期的模拟赛中发现，bot很容易点炮，尤其是给对方大牌点炮时的失分较多。在强调得分效率的竞争环境中，防守不足会直接抵消进攻收益。
同时，普通行为克隆会学习所有动作，但即使来自第一名Bot，不同轨迹的长期收益也并不相同，其中还包括不利局面下的被迫选择。因此，问题不只是“能否模仿”，还包括“哪些动作值得模仿得更多”。

## 第 4 页：整体框架与三个创新点

这一页是决赛bot整个系统的全貌。原始牌谱首先被转换成四名玩家的决策轨迹，并根据最终得分生成不同的样本权重。牌面状态进入 ResNet，真实弃牌顺序则进入 GRU，融合后得到合法动作空间上的策略。推理阶段只做轻量调整，最后输出合法动作。策略强度则通过固定牌墙和座位轮换进行配对评测。

本项目主要有三个创新点。

第一，训练上保留四家轨迹，但根据结果降低低收益和点炮轨迹的影响。
第二，风险不是全局统一估计，而是区分三个对手，并保留弃牌的真实时间顺序。
第三，对于最终采取策略的评估不依赖一次胜率，而是在控制牌墙和座位后计算配对置信区间。

## 第 5 页：结果加权训练

这一页给出了结果加权的具体形式。终局得分通过单调函数把得分转换成权重。如果某名玩家最终点炮，其样本权重还会进一步降低。

输家在危险局面中正确弃和的样本依旧被保留，低收益动作的权重降低。

## 第 6 页：逐对手、带时序的风险判断

早期系统曾把“某个对手打过的牌”近似看成对所有人都安全，这会低估另外两家的风险。最终系统分别分析三名对手的牌河、副露、听牌概率和潜在损失。

同时，我们保存桌面上真实交错的弃牌顺序，而不是把四条牌河简单拼接。较新的弃牌证据权重更高，副露之后的弃牌也单独处理。推理阶段只会在危险局面下，对模型分数接近的候选弃牌做小幅调整。

## 第 7 页：训练过程

这页展示了监督训练的验证损失曲线。验证损失从约 0.77 降到 0.34，在第 17 个验证点附近趋于饱和。

## 第 8 页：配对评测方法

以上是bot的架构与特点，接下来是对策略进行本地评测的介绍。麻将评测最大的困难是方差。抽到的牌墙和所在座位不同，可能比策略差异本身影响更大。

因此我们固定 800 个牌墙，让每个策略分别坐遍四个位置，每个策略一共得到 3,200 局结果。两个策略在相同条件下逐局配对，再对得分差进行一万次 Bootstrap 重采样，以平均得分差作为主要统计量，结果报告 95% 置信区间。

## 第 9 页：主要性能结果

PPT上展示了最终策略的测试数据。最终策略的平均得分提升较高，和牌率上升同时点炮率下降。也就是说，这个策略使bot的整体性能有较大的提升。

## 第 10 页：消融实验说明收益来自哪里

我们还测试了动作价值、期望损失和番型路线等辅助控制。虽然全部启用时的点估计略高一些，但提升不大，增量置信区间没有给出可靠优势，因此最终决赛时没有采用。

## 第 11 页：比赛结果与结论边界

优化后的系统在 16 支队伍参加的决赛中获得第九名，并且离线单次决策平均约为 2.37 毫秒，满足实时对局需要。

但当前模型尚未在统一数据和评测协议下与所有外部方法比较。项目的价值集中于通过监督学习与轻量微调，以较低成本提升 Bot 的实战能力。
## 第 12 页：总结

最后总结一下。这个项目最重要的经验是让监督学习知道哪些行为更值得模仿。

通过结果加权，我们保留了有价值的防守样本，并降低了低收益和点炮轨迹的干扰；通过逐对手时序风险建模，我们提高了对危险局面的辨别能力；通过固定牌墙和座位轮换，我们获得了更可信的性能证据。

接下来的工作是进一步扩展训练数据与增强方式，根据局面自适应调节进攻强度，并以当前监督策略为起点开展自博弈微调，探索强化学习方案。

以下是项目的参考文献。最后感谢IJCAI与比赛主办方的支持与帮助，感谢李文新教授在人工智能基础课程上的指导。
我的分享到此结束，谢谢大家。

# English Presentation Script

## Slide 1: Title

Hello everyone. I am Xue Zhiyao from Peking University. Today, I would like to introduce my Chinese Standard Mahjong Bot.

## Slide 2: Baseline Architecture

Let me first introduce the basic architecture, which was also used by our Bot in the points event. The training data consist of self-play game logs generated by the champion Bot from the 2020 competition. Each decision state is encoded as a 70-channel tensor over a 4×9 tile grid—covering hand information, public discards, risk indicators, and progress features—as summarized on the slide.

A ResNet extracts tile combinations and visible state information. Separate policy heads then predict the action type and the specific tile. Finally, a legal-action mask is applied over the 235-dimensional action space, ensuring that every output can be executed directly.

## Slide 3: Why Ordinary Behavior Cloning Is Not Enough

However, improving the Bot from the points event to the final remained difficult. In our early simulations, the Bot dealt in too frequently, and the losses were especially costly when an opponent completed a high-value hand. In a competitive setting that rewards scoring efficiency, weak defense can directly cancel the gains from aggressive play.

Ordinary behavior cloning also imitates every recorded action. Even when all trajectories come from a champion Bot, they can have very different long-term returns and may include forced choices in unfavorable situations. Therefore, the question is not only whether the Bot can imitate, but also which behavior deserves more weight.

## Slide 4: Overall Framework and Three Main Contributions

This slide shows the complete framework of the final Bot. The original game logs are first converted into decision trajectories for all four players. Different sample weights are assigned according to the final scores. The tile-based state is processed by a ResNet, while the true discard sequence is processed by a GRU. Their representations are fused to produce a policy over the legal action space. At inference time, only lightweight adjustments are applied.

The project has three main contributions.

First, we retain trajectories from all four players, but reduce the influence of low-return and deal-in trajectories.

Second, risk is estimated separately for each of the three opponents, while preserving the true temporal order of discards.

Third, the final policy is not judged by a single win rate. We control the walls and seats and report paired confidence intervals.

I will elaborate on these points in the following slides.
## Slide 5: Outcome-Weighted Training

This slide summarizes the outcome-weighting method. The terminal score is transformed into a sample weight through a monotonic function. If a player eventually deals in, the weight is reduced further.

Importantly, useful defensive decisions made by non-winning players are still retained, while low-return behavior receives less weight.

## Slide 6: Opponent-Specific Temporal Risk

An early version of the system approximately treated a discarded tile as absolutely safe,which may underestimate the risk posed by the other two players. The final system analyzes the river, exposed melds, tenpai probability, and potential loss of each opponent separately.

We also preserve the true interleaved discard order at the table instead of simply concatenating four discard rivers. More recent evidence receives greater weight, and discards after an exposed meld are handled separately. 
## Slide 7: Training Process

This slide shows the validation-loss curve during supervised training. The validation loss decreased from approximately 0.7 to 0.3 and began to saturate near the seventeenth validation point.

## Slide 8: Paired Evaluation

So far, I have introduced the architecture and the main algorithmic changes. I will now briefly explain our local evaluation method. The main difficulty in Mahjong evaluation is variance. Differences in the wall and seat can have a larger effect than the difference between two policies.

We therefore fixed 800 walls and evaluated each policy in all four seats, producing 3,200 games per policy. The two policies were paired game by game under the same conditions. We then performed 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the score differences, using the mean score difference as the primary statistic and reporting a 95 percent confidence interval.

## Slide 9: Main Performance Results

The slide shows the evaluation results of the final policy. Its average score increased substantially. At the same time, the win rate increased and the deal-in rate decreased. In other words, the policy improved the Bot's overall offense-defense trade-off.

## Slide 10: What the Ablation Study Shows

We also tested auxiliary controls based on action value, expected loss, and fan routes. Although enabling all of them produced a slightly higher point estimate, the improvement was small, and the incremental confidence intervals did not show a reliable advantage. We therefore disabled these controls in the final competition configuration.

## Slide 11: Competition Result and Scope of the Claim

The optimized system placed ninth among sixteen teams in the final. Its average offline decision time also satisfies the real-time requirement of gameplay.

However, the model has not been compared with every external method under a unified dataset and evaluation protocol. The contribution of this project is therefore a practical and relatively low-cost way to improve the playing strength of a supervised-learning Bot through better supervision and lightweight refinement.

## Slide 12: Conclusion

To conclude, the most important lesson from this project is that supervised learning should distinguish which behavior deserves more imitation.

Outcome weighting preserves useful defensive examples while reducing interference from low-return and deal-in trajectories. Opponent-specific temporal risk modeling improves the recognition of dangerous situations. Fixed-wall evaluation with seat rotation provides more credible performance evidence.

For future work, we plan to expand the training data and augmentation methods, adapt the level of aggression to the game state, and explore self-play reinforcement learning initialized from the current supervised policy.
